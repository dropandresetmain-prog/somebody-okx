# Somebody Brand Guide

## Locked identity

**Product:** Somebody  
**Tagline:** **Somebody has to do it. Now Somebody can.**

## Core thesis

Small teams constantly generate messy operational work that does not cleanly belong to anyone. Someone says “Can somebody sort this out?” and the work lands on whoever has capacity.

Somebody is the autonomous AI coworker that takes ownership of that bounded operational work, drives it forward across apps, information and external people, and returns to the human when judgment or authority is required.

Procurement is the first demonstrated job, not the long-term product category.

Reliability is the mechanism that makes Somebody safe and dependable, not the headline positioning.

## Character direction

Somebody is a small ambiguous non-human office creature.

Personality:

- mildly annoyed, never angry;
- extremely competent;
- extremely dependable;
- fast;
- calm under mess;
- quietly accustomed to cleaning up work other people left behind.

Humour comes from the situation, not from sarcasm or incompetence.

The intended feeling is:

> I would rather not be cleaning this up, but obviously I will.

## Visual language

Locked traits:

- compact rounded body;
- simple, recognizable silhouette;
- warm off-white / pale beige body tone;
- two small dark hair-like tufts on top;
- half-lidded dark eyes;
- restrained mouth and brow movement;
- short rounded arms and feet;
- no human face, skin, hair or realistic anatomy;
- minimal office props;
- premium 3D-character / soft-rendered look rather than flat clip art;
- readable at favicon, sticker and presentation scale.

Somebody may perform human office actions such as:

- typing on a laptop;
- drinking coffee;
- checking paperwork;
- reviewing quotes;
- holding a phone;
- following up;
- waiting for replies;
- asking for approval;
- verifying completion.

## Expression range

Primary expressions:

- neutral;
- focused;
- mildly annoyed;
- concerned;
- satisfied;
- happy, used rarely.

Avoid exaggerated rage, despair, smugness, snark, laziness or slapstick.

## Brand voice

Product copy should be calm, competent and concise.

Useful language territory:

- Give Somebody a job
- What needs handling?
- Somebody is on it
- Somebody is working
- Somebody found something
- Somebody needs your decision
- What Somebody found
- Why Somebody recommends this
- Needs your approval
- Done
- Done and verified

Do not force the name “Somebody” into every sentence.

## Do not drift into

- a human office worker;
- a specific real animal;
- a direct imitation of Gudetama, Pusheen, Aggretsuko, Rilakkuma, Sumikko Gurashi or Grumpy Cat;
- hyper-cute baby proportions;
- angry or cynical mascot behaviour;
- incompetent/lazy comic relief;
- overly detailed clothing;
- mascot-first interfaces where the character overwhelms operational information.

## Canonical asset plan

Preserve the approved concept board as the visual source of truth and derive reusable transparent-background assets from it.

Target asset set:

1. neutral / standing;
2. working on laptop;
3. coffee;
4. searching / inspecting;
5. phone / chasing update;
6. waiting;
7. needs approval;
8. done;
9. done and verified;
10. head-only icon / avatar;
11. wide hero illustration.

Future generations should use an approved canonical Somebody asset as a visual reference rather than regenerating the character from scratch.
